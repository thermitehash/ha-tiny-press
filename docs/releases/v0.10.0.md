# v0.10.0 — Holidays and themes

The morning slip recognizes special days, and puzzles follow the season or today's holiday.

## Added
- **Holiday banners on the morning slip.** On a recognized day, a small piece of ASCII art and a one-line description print under the date, for example "Diwali is the festival of lights…". Days are recognized on the day itself only, with no countdowns. Other days recognized the same day print as "Also today: …".
- **Configure → Holidays and themes:**
  - **Everyday celebrations, on by default:**
    - seasons and solstices, which follow your hemisphere
    - fun science days, such as Pi Day, Earth Day, World Oceans Day, Space Day and Moon Day
    - public holidays from any Holidays integration calendar you pick
    - a first day of school you set
  - **Holiday groups, all off by default and all treated the same way:**
    - East Asian festivals
    - Jewish, Muslim, Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist and Christian holidays
    - Persian New Year
    - Cultural and heritage days
    - Fun days

    After you pick groups, you can untick single days.
  - **Family days** (Configure → Add a family day): birthdays, name days or local festivals, every year on the same date, with your own banner text and a choice of art.
  - **Puzzle theme:** Auto (today's holiday, else the season), Off, or a fixed theme such as Space or Ocean.
- **Themed prints:**
  - **Word searches:** take up to a third of their words from the theme, and kid interests still mix in.
  - **Crosswords:** get up to four themed answers with clues.
  - **Mazes:** show the theme's art and name.
  - **Jokes and facts:** on a recognized day that has its own (such as Pi Day jokes, or NASA facts on Moon Day), that's what prints, including on the morning slip.
- **Quote from a feed:** the bottom's content source can now be **A feed**. The quote comes from the newest item in the Quote feed you pick (its summary is the quote and its title the author), with bundled quotes as the fallback.

## Content
- Banners, art, word lists, clues and the new jokes were written for Tiny Press. Descriptions stay neutral and factual.
- Moving dates for 2026–2035 come from the python-holidays library's calendars (MIT). Islamic dates are calculated and can differ by a day where the moon is sighted locally. Vesak's date varies by country (Indonesia's is used). Holidays that begin at sundown are shown on their first full day.
